## Why this task

Structure determination from powder diffraction (SDPD) is the job a solid-state chemist does when a compound will not grow a single crystal: pigments, most polymorphs discovered in screening, salts and hydrates precipitated from process streams. The input is a single one-dimensional trace; the output is a complete crystal structure. It is chemistry in the payroll sense (every pharma solid-form group and every pigment manufacturer pays for it) and it is a different capability from spectroscopic structure elucidation (e.g. from 2D NMR). There the unknown is the molecule and the data carry a strong self-check (shift prediction). Here the molecule is known, the unknown is how it packs in three dimensions, and the problem is an inverse projection: recover a periodic 3D arrangement from a 1D projection with heavy peak overlap. Algorithmically it is docking's cousin (rigid fragments plus torsions optimised against a scoring function) sitting on top of a lattice-recovery step (indexing) that is the same "find a basis from noisy inner products" problem cryptographers and number theorists know from lattice reduction. The fit statistic gives a weak verifier with false positives, which is exactly the regime where your saturation finding does not apply: the model cannot rebuild the oracle because the oracle is the deposited structure, not a simulation.

The reason to expect strong (multiplicative) failure rather than weak failure: the job is a chain of independent decisions — background and peak picking, indexing among several candidate cells with similar figures of merit, space group from extinctions, Pawley extraction, building a Z-matrix with the right ring conformations and protonation, choosing Z and Z′, running a global search that for >20 DoF succeeds in a small fraction of runs, recognising a false minimum, and refining with restraints. Errors occur in both directions (confident wrong cells, and correct cells abandoned), so a failure analysis will not be one-directional.

## The task in five sentences

The agent receives, for each of 30 instances, one measured laboratory powder X-ray diffraction pattern (angle, intensity, uncertainty), the instrument facts needed to model it (wavelength, geometry, temperature, sample mounting), and the chemical identity of what is in the sample (SMILES of each component with stoichiometry, e.g. one drug cation, one hydrogen-sulfate anion, one propan-2-ol per formula unit). Nothing about the unit cell, space group, number of molecules per cell or any coordinates is given. The agent must infer the crystal lattice, the symmetry, and the position, orientation and conformation of every molecule and ion, i.e. build the crystal structure that produced the pattern. It submits one CIF per instance containing the cell, the space group and the coordinates of all non-hydrogen atoms in the asymmetric unit, plus a small JSON with the reduced cell and space-group symbol. The submission is checked against the deposited crystal structure of the same phase, which the agent never sees.

## The grader

Two scientific units per instance, both binary, fixed weights, no partial credit inside a unit.

**Unit L (lattice and symmetry).** Passes when the Niggli-reduced cell of the submission matches the Niggli-reduced reference cell within a stated tolerance and the space-group type is the same as the reference (any setting; enantiomorphic pairs such as P4₁/P4₃ treated as one type). Proposed tolerance: lengths within 1 %, angles within 1°. Basis: differences between laboratory-powder and single-crystal cells of the same phase are dominated by temperature and zero-shift and sit at a few tenths of a percent, while wrong indexing solutions (sub/supercells, wrong crystal system) differ by far more; the team validates the exact numbers against the source papers' reported cells and the single-crystal cells where both exist. This unit is a real professional deliverable on its own — the indexed cell and space group are what go into a polymorph patent claim and what a Pawley-based quantification needs.

**Unit S (structure).** Passes when the submitted structure, after symmetry expansion and a symmetry-aware periodic match against the reference (pymatgen-style structure matching or an equivalent implemented by the team), places every non-hydrogen atom of the asymmetric unit within tolerance: proposed RMSD over non-H atoms ≤ 0.35 Å and no single atom displaced more than 1.0 Å, so a flipped substituent or a mis-oriented ring fails. Basis for the tolerance: the DASH benchmark study that redetermined 35 molecular structures from laboratory data reports RMSDs between the powder solutions and independent single-crystal structures from 0.017 to 0.204 Å with a mean of 0.093 ± 0.043 Å, including temperature-mismatch contributions (RMSD ranged from 0.017 Å to 0.204 Å across all 35 structures, with a mean of 0.093 ± 0.043 Å); 0.35 Å is well outside that spread and far inside the ≥1 Å errors of any wrong packing. Identity dependency: the CIF must contain a heavy-atom set whose perceived connectivity matches the supplied SMILES and stoichiometry; otherwise S is invalid (a blob of atoms with a good fit is not a structure). Hydrogens are ignored. For achiral formula units in Sohncke groups both hands are accepted (powder data cannot fix the crystal's handedness); for chiral molecules the supplied stereochemistry must be preserved. S can pass while L fails (a correct arrangement described in a subgroup such as P1 is still the correct structure); L can pass while S fails (indexed but unsolved). That is the intended partial-progress ladder.

**Weights and aggregate.** Each instance carries three points: one for L, two for S. The benchmark score is points earned divided by 90. Zero means no instance was even indexed correctly; one third is the easy-only ceiling (every cell and space group right, no structure solved); one means all 30 structures are correct. Report the raw counts of L and S passes, and S passes broken down by DoF bin, next to the score. This is fixed-weight exact correctness with no departures; the 1:2 weight is justified before any run by the professional roles (the structure is the deliverable, the cell is a useful intermediate deliverable), not by any model's failures. No human floor is measured; label the README "ground-truth normalised, no human floor measured" per your Sep 4 doctrine, with per-instance proof of solvability coming from the source paper (below).

**Parser and edge behaviour.** Missing CIF → both units zero for that instance. More than one candidate CIF for an instance → that instance scores zero (hedging buys nothing). Malformed CIF but valid JSON cell → L can still be scored, S is zero. Reference unavailable or matcher dependency failure → UNAVAILABLE, no numeric reward. Deterministic: the matching is exact under the declared rule.

**Arithmetic checks.** Ten instances indexed correctly, three of those solved, no others: 10 + 6 = 16 points → 0.178. Thirty correct cells, zero structures: 30 points → 0.333. A structure with the right cell and the right molecule orientation but one aryl ring rotated 60° (one atom off by 1.3 Å): L passes, S fails, 1 point — closeness is diagnostic only.

Pseudocode in words: for each instance, parse JSON cell and CIF; compute Niggli cell and space-group type via spglib; L = cell within tolerance AND same group type. If the CIF parses and its heavy-atom composition and perceived bonds match the given SMILES set, expand both structures to P1, run the symmetry-aware matcher in the reference setting, compute per-atom displacements under the best mapping; S = RMSD and max displacement within tolerance. Sum points, divide by 90, emit the ledger with per-instance distances.

## Professional value and end-to-end work

The person is a solid-state or pharmaceutical crystallographer; the artifact is a CIF for a solid form that would not give a single crystal; the downstream uses are polymorph patents, regulatory solid-form characterisation, crystal-structure-prediction validation, and property calculation (solubility, stability, morphology). The good-practice guide published this year by the DASH/GALLOP authors is a step-by-step account of exactly this workflow (a step-by-step outline for academic and industrial practitioners determining molecular organic crystal structures from powder X-ray diffraction data), and it notes that the technique's importance was cemented by pharmaceutical polymorph patent disputes, with APIs' low symmetry and large cells producing heavily overlapped patterns (the 1990s marked a turning point for SDPD, as patent disputes over pharmaceutical polymorphs highlighted its value when single crystals were unavailable; the low symmetry and large unit cells of APIs often result in heavily overlapped PXRD patterns).

Steps the agent must perform, all inside the run: read and validate the pattern; fit background and pick peaks; index (choose among candidate cells); determine the space group from extinctions and Z from volume increments; extract intensities by Pawley or Le Bail fit; build a molecular model with the right conformers, protonation and fragment count; run a direct-space global search; judge convergence and false minima; Rietveld-refine with restraints; write a CIF. The inputs contain none of this — the molecular connectivity is what the chemist always knows and does not define the answer.

Expert effort, evidence-backed: in the DASH benchmark study, every compound needed 20 simulated-annealing runs of 10⁷ moves after indexing, and the hardest case needed batches of at least 50 runs with 2×10⁷ moves and a slowed cooling rate before it solved reproducibly (the structure was solved reproducibly by combining a bigger batch size of at least 50 runs and an increased number of SA moves per run with a reduced cooling rate). A Z′ = 3, 30-DoF structure was reported solved by distributing DASH runs across a 360-vCPU cloud cluster (a 360 vCPU cluster returned the crystal structure of racemic ornidazole, Z′=3, 30 degrees of freedom). When indexing fails, the from-scratch alternative costs about 12 hours per global-optimisation run in a single space group on a desktop CPU, and one pigment needed 28 such runs across nine crystal symmetries (a typical full global optimization run in one space group with 10⁶ random trial structures took about 12 hours, range 3–30 hours, on an Intel Core i7). Read as a workflow, a 20–35 DoF salt or solvate from laboratory data is multiple working days of a trained crystallographer's active decisions (indexing attempts, symmetry hypotheses, model building, several search batches, restrained refinement), which I label as an estimate supported by those records rather than a measured baseline.

## Private reference, coverage and input sufficiency

Reference category: deposited crystal structures determined by the source authors from the same powder data (Rietveld-refined), which is a scientific model rather than a direct measurement, hardened in two ways. First, selection restricts the panel to structures validated independently: van de Streek and Neumann energy-minimised 215 organic powder structures from IUCr journals with dispersion-corrected DFT and found that almost 9 percent, 19 of the 215 powder structures, were demonstrably in error, with seven showing a minor error for a non-H atom, five where minimisation suggests higher space-group symmetry, and preferred orientation the most important cause of problems; the panel takes only entries their table passes, excludes the higher-symmetry cases from Unit L entirely, and excludes disordered references. Second, wherever an independent single-crystal determination of the same phase exists (the 35-compound DASH set all have single-crystal references; the GALLOP repository ships the raw verapamil hydrochloride and famotidine patterns from that set), the single-crystal structure is the primary reference and the powder structure is the secondary check.

Reference coverage: L and S cover the whole advertised deliverable except hydrogen positions and protonation state, which are declared unassessed. Sufficiency: each instance's own paper is the completion witness — the structure was solved from that pattern with a named program, stated DoF and stated number of runs, and the instrument and composition information given to the agent are the same information the authors had. Multiple valid answers: setting choices, origin shifts and enantiomorph choice for achiral units are declared equivalences; distinct polymorphs are not.

## Difficulty dossier

**Dated regime.** Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra as of September 2026, agentic with code execution, offline, 4 vCPU, no GPU, 8 h wall clock, the open tools above.

**Competent automation review.** The best specialist tooling, with the cell, space group and a Z-matrix taken from the known reference structure, gives this profile: for compounds with DOF < 15, correct solutions were generated in ∼100% of SA runs; for the more complex structures with DOF ≥ 15, local minima reduce the frequency of success and widen the χ² spread; the 22-DoF verapamil hydrochloride returned only one solution in 20 runs, and the authors place structures beyond 20 DoF at the current state of the art of SDPD from laboratory XRPD data. A 2025 IUCr paper puts the practical ceiling of direct-space methods at about 40 DoF and stresses sample quality as the binding constraint (the current limitation on the complexity of DS methods, quantified by DOF, remains at approximately 40; the efficacy is significantly constrained by the quality of the diffraction pattern). GALLOP improves search frequency by an order of magnitude over DASH but is GPU-built (using GPUs to accelerate the calculations; in both cases the frequency of solutions was improved by an order of magnitude relative to DASH). Upstream of the search, indexing is still the step experts call the bottleneck: Schmidt's group built an entire from-scratch method because reliable indexing fails if the pattern contains too few or too broad reflections or if the powder is not phase-pure, and Harris's commentary frames combining cell determination with structure solution as the way around cases for which conventional approaches for indexing powder XRD data prove to be challenging. ML end-to-end solvers exist for inorganics and simulated data only; none handles flexible organics from experimental laboratory patterns. No published result of an LLM agent attempting SDPD exists to my knowledge.

**The specific bottleneck that survives automation.** Three things, all scientific: choosing the right cell and space group when several candidates fit the peak positions comparably (a wrong choice is fatal and self-verification does not catch it until much later); building the right model (ring conformers, which fragment is protonated, how many waters, Z′ > 1) so that the search space contains the answer at all — note that the published success statistics above used Z-matrices generated from the reference crystal structure itself, which the agent cannot do; and recognising the global minimum among false ones when the search succeeds only in a few percent of runs, on a 4-CPU budget where each 2×10⁷-move run costs real time.

**Applicable current-model evidence and transfer limits.** In earlier evaluations by the same team, current models scored low on tasks whose score-bearing unit is a coupled 3D construction (identity plus placement) and high on deduction tasks with a strong built-in verifier. SDPD sits on the 3D-construction side: the score-bearing unit is a global 3D construction whose correctness depends on every upstream decision. The deduction result says models handle deduction with a strong verifier; here the verifier (profile χ²) is weak and only available after the expensive step. Transfer limit: none of these is the same modality, so this is a reasoned forecast, not an observed failure on this instance set.

**Easy-only ceiling and weights.** The routine pipeline (auto-index, extinction symbol, default FOX runs with an RDKit conformer) is the comparator the team should run on every instance; its expected outcome is a fraction of L passes and S passes concentrated in the <15 DoF calibration slice. The one-third easy-only ceiling is visible by construction.

**Panel design.** Thirty instances from the validated pool: about six at 10–15 DoF (the calibration ladder), fourteen at 15–25 DoF, ten at 25–40 DoF or Z′ ≥ 2 or salt/solvate with independent counter-ion and solvent fragments. Laboratory data only (Cu Kα1, the regime the difficulty evidence describes); exclude nanocrystalline pigments whose truth needed ssNMR/PDF to disambiguate (Schmidt's difluoro-quinacridone case), exclude disordered references, and exclude compounds whose cells are famous enough to be recalled (paracetamol, carbamazepine, ibuprofen): the source pool from the 2014 list plus post-2014 Acta C/E/IUCrData reports (Chernyshev's pharma salts and solvates, Schmidt's pigments, Shankland's cocrystals) is large enough to avoid them.

**Strongest route by which an agent could succeed.** A fully scripted pipeline plus recalled torsion priors, run in parallel on 4 cores for 8 hours, will plausibly solve the <15 DoF slice and index a fair share of the rest; that is the intended curve. Memorised unit cells are the one shortcut that bypasses real work for Unit L; the team runs a recall probe (ask the model, offline, for the cell and space group given only the composition) and drops or flags any instance it recalls.

**Refutation.** The judgment is wrong if the frozen pilot passes S on a third or more of the panel with at least half of those at ≥ 20 DoF, or if the routine no-judgment comparator alone solves a fifth of the panel — in which case the affected instances are saturated and the panel moves to higher DoF, Z′ > 1 and lower data quality rather than banning tools.

## Anti-shortcut checks and the fixtures the team runs

Reasoned expectations, to be executed by implementation: the deposited reference CIF re-submitted passes L and S on every instance; the single-crystal structure of the same phase (where available) passes S, which validates the tolerance from the other side; a different polymorph or solvate of the same molecule fails both; the reference described in P1 with all symmetry-generated molecules passes S and fails L; the reference re-expressed in an alternative setting or shifted origin passes both; the mirror image passes for achiral units in Sohncke groups and fails for chiral molecules; the reference with one ring rotated (max displacement 1.3 Å, RMSD 0.3 Å) fails S; the reference jittered by 0.2 Å passes and by 0.6 Å fails; a correct cell with random coordinates passes L only; the correct cell with swapped molecule positions in a Z′ = 2 case passes L only; a CIF whose composition does not match the SMILES is invalid for S; two CIFs for one instance score zero; a missing instance scores zero; a corrupt reference returns UNAVAILABLE; repeated grading is identical. Also run the 19 structures flagged as erroneous in the 2014 validation against their own references as a defective battery: they should fail S, which is the strongest evidence the tolerance is neither loose nor arbitrary.

## One-container execution contract (proposed, awaiting profiling)

One objective, one panel of 30 instances, one solver container with all inputs present at launch, no network, 4 vCPU, 16 GB RAM, ~5 GB disk, no GPU, 8 h wall clock enforced by the host, final artifact set `submission/<id>.cif` and `submission/<id>.json`, evaluator outside the container with references and matcher, runtime of the evaluator under a minute per instance, UNAVAILABLE on evaluator failure, replacement only for demonstrated infrastructure faults with the original preserved. The decisive resource check: for every candidate instance, run FOX (and GALLOP on CPU) with the correct cell, space group and an RDKit/xtb-built Z-matrix on the same 4-vCPU box and record time-to-solution and success frequency; keep only instances whose oracle-assisted solve lands within about two hours so that failure in the real run is failure of inference, not of compute; if that leaves too few instances above 20 DoF, raise the box to 8–16 vCPU for this environment instead of keeping compute-impossible cases.

## Objections, limits, and rejected leads

The reference for most instances is a fit to the same data rather than an independent measurement; that is why the panel is restricted to DFT-D-validated entries and why single-crystal structures are used as primary references wherever they exist. The self-verification available through profile statistics is real but weak — the hardest published case (four structural models with similar patterns, settled only with ssNMR, PDF and DFT-D) shows the fit alone can be ambiguous — and that class is excluded from the panel precisely so that the truth is not in doubt. Memorisation of unit cells for well-known drugs is the one bypass that needs an active probe. The compute question is handled by the oracle-assisted profiling above, and it is the only place the difficulty claim could quietly become a budget artifact.


**Recommendation:** implement. Acquisition and build order: rsync the COD `hkl` directory and grep for `_pd_` profile loops, join with CIF metadata, keep organic entries, compute DoF (rotatable bonds from the CIF-derived SMILES plus independent fragments), intersect with the 2014 validation list and post-2014 IUCr reports, exclude disorder, higher-symmetry flags and famous compounds, download the `.rtv` files from the journal as primary source, strip every calculated column, build the image with FOX/GSAS-II/pymatgen/spglib/RDKit/xtb, implement the matcher, run the fixture battery and the defective battery, run the routine comparator and the oracle-assisted timing on the frozen box, run the recall probe, freeze, then one pilot per model.
